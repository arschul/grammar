Reduced
Relative Clauses

Storm chasers near the tornado can report in real time.

drop the pronoun drop the pronoun + be
The idea

Same clause, fewer words

A defining relative clause can lose its pronoun — and sometimes the verb be as well. What is left still describes the same noun.

Full → reduced

People who are interested in storm chasing can stream this interview.

People interested in storm chasing can stream this interview.

What disappears

× the relative pronoun

× sometimes be

Nothing is added, nothing moves. Words are only deleted.

Pattern 1 · 2 · 3

What follows the pronoun?

Look at the words straight after who, which or that. They decide what you can delete.

A new subject
drop the pronoun

tornadoes that we have seen
→ tornadoes we've seen or heard about

be + adjective
drop pronoun + be

People who are interested in…
→ People interested in storm chasing

be + preposition
drop pronoun + be

chasers who are near the tornado
→ chasers near the tornado

One step further

Participle clauses

Delete the pronoun and the tense, and you are left with an -ing form (active) or a past participle (passive).

the man who is standing over there
the letters that were sent yesterday ← passive
anyone who wants a ticket → anyone wanting a ticket
The limit

When you can't reduce

If the pronoun is the subject of its clause and no form of be follows it, the pronoun has to stay.

Works

the film which Tom recommended

→ the film Tom recommended

Doesn't work

the man who lives next door

→ the man lives next door (no subject left)

⚠ Watch out

Common mistakes

The man lives next door is a pilot.

Correct: the man who lives next door…

People are interested in it can stream it.

Correct: people interested in it…

My brother, I saw yesterday, has moved.

Correct: my brother, who I saw yesterday…

the letters sending yesterday

Correct: the letters sent yesterday.

✓ The pronoun is the subject and no be follows, so it stays.

People interested in it can stream this.

✓ The pronoun and be go together, never just one.

My brother, I saw yesterday, has moved.

My brother, who I saw yesterday, has moved.

✓ Non-defining clauses (with commas) keep the pronoun.

The letters sent last week arrived today.
