Relative
Clauses

The engineer who designs new software isn't here — but my boss, who is very nice, is.

defining non-defining
The idea

Adding information to a noun

A relative clause comes straight after the noun it describes. The question is whether the information is essential or extra.

Examples

I like products that use recycled materials.

My phone, which cost a lot, isn't working.

The test

essential → no commas

extra → commas

Remove the clause: is the sentence still complete?

Type 1

Defining clauses

Essential information — it identifies the noun. No commas. who, that, which, where, when, why

The engineer who designs new software isn't here.
I like products that use ethically sourced materials.
I like products (that) I can use again and again.  object — can be omitted
Type 2

Non-defining clauses

Extra information — the sentence works without it. Commas always. Only who and which — never that.

My phone, which cost a lot, isn't working properly.
My boss, who is very nice, is retiring next year.
My boss, who I have known for years, is a good friend.  never omitted here
Punctuation matters

The comma changes the meaning

no commas
defining

My sister who lives in Rome is a doctor.

I have more than one sister. This is the Rome one.

commas
non-defining

My sister, who lives in Rome, is a doctor.

I have one sister. Rome is just extra detail.

Commas come in pairs — one before the clause, one after it.

⚠ Watch out

Common mistakes

My phone, that cost a lot, broke.

Correct: My phone, which cost a lot, broke.

The people which live upstairs are quiet.

Correct: The people who live upstairs are quiet.

The man who he works here is my cousin.

Correct: The man who works here is my cousin.

That's the village which I was born.

Correct: the village where I was born.

Spot the mistake

My phone, that cost a lot, isn't working properly.

My phone, which cost a lot, isn't working properly.

✓ That never appears in a non-defining clause.

The man who he works in the lab is my cousin.

The man who works in the lab is my cousin.

✓ Who is already the subject — drop he.

The book was written by a journalist which I admire.

The book was written by a journalist who I admire.

✓ Use who or that for people, never which.

The woman lives next door is a doctor.

The woman who lives next door is a doctor.

✓ A subject pronoun can never be left out.
