Paired
Conjunctions

It is neither a passive nor a formulaic process.

two halves, always keep them parallel
The idea

Two halves, one job

Paired conjunctions link two things using two words that belong together. You can never use one half without the other.

Examples

You should either turn your phone off or leave it in another room.

Creativity not only varies between people but also varies across your life.

The four pairs

both … and

either … or

neither … nor

not only … but also

Rule 1

What each pair does

both … and — the two things are equally important
either … or — a choice between two possibilities
neither … nor — joins two negative options
not only … but also — the second thing is surprising or adds weight
Rule 2

Keep the halves parallel

Whatever form follows the first half must also follow the second: noun with noun, -ing with -ing, adjective with adjective.

✓ both having ideas and putting them into practice
✓ The course is both interesting and useful.
✗ The course is both interesting and it is useful. (adjective + clause)
Rule 3

Which verb form?

When the pair joins two subjects, the verb doesn't always agree with both.

both … and

Always plural.

Both my sister and my brother live in Canada.

either · neither · not only

Agrees with the nearer subject.

Neither the manager nor the assistants were told.

⚠ Watch out

Common mistakes

You can either take the bus nor walk.

Correct: either take the bus or walk.

It is both interesting and it is useful.

Correct: both interesting and useful.

I don't like neither tea nor coffee.

Correct: I like neither tea nor coffee.

Both my sister and my brother lives abroad.

Correct: both … and takes live.
