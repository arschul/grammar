Verb Patterns

Verb + Object + Infinitive

The pattern

Many verbs are followed by an object and then a to-infinitive. The object is the person who actually performs the infinitive action — that is what makes this pattern different from a plain verb + infinitive.

verb + object + to + base verb
We encourage visitors to stay here.
The doctor told him to rest for a week.
Did they invite you to speak at the conference?
Compare: I want to go. — I go.  |  I want you to go. — you go. The object changes who does the action.

Which verbs take this pattern?

These are the most common ones. It helps to group them by what they do.

FunctionVerbsExample
influencingadvise, encourage, persuade, force, getShe persuaded me to apply.
permittingallow, permitThey don't allow guests to park here.
requestingask, tell, invite, warnHe asked us to wait outside.
wantingwant, would like, expect, needI need you to sign this.
Two exceptions: make and let take an object + bare infinitive (no to): They made him leave. She let me borrow it. But in the passive, make recovers its to: He was made to leave.

Two negatives, two meanings

You can make the sentence negative in two places, and the meaning is not the same. Put not before the infinitive to negate the action; make the main verb negative to negate the influence or the expectation.

verb + object + not to + base verb   ·   negative verb + object + to + base verb
We ask them not to contact us by phone. → the request is: don't phone
We don't expect everyone to like all our ideas. → we have no such expectation
Watch the difference: I didn't tell her to come (I said nothing) is not the same as I told her not to come (I said: don't come).

Reporting what people said

This pattern is the natural way to report instructions, advice, requests, warnings and invitations without repeating the speaker's exact words. Choose the reporting verb that captures the speaker's purpose, then add the object and the infinitive.

What was saidReported
"Check in two hours early."The agent advised me to check in two hours early.
"Don't walk on the grass!"The gardener told Elizabeth not to walk on the grass.
"Would you like to come with me?"Jane invited Nelly to go with her.
"You mustn't stay longer than 90 days."The official warned Edwin not to stay longer.
⚠ Common mistakes:
✗ The gardener told to Elizabeth... → ✓ told Elizabeth... (no to before the person)
✗ They allowed me stay. → ✓ allowed me to stay.
✗ Her father wants that she studies medicine. → ✓ wants her to study medicine.
✗ She asked him don't call her. → ✓ asked him not to call her.
✗ They made him to leave. → ✓ made him leave. (bare infinitive after make)
✗ My teacher suggested me to take the course. → ✓ suggested that I take the course. (suggest never uses this pattern)
