The idea

Same facts, new spotlight

Cleft means split. We break one sentence into two parts so that a single element gets the emphasis. The information does not change.

Plain → cleft

A love of life motivates them.

It's a love of life that motivates them.

The engine

the spotlight sits next to be

the rest becomes a clause

Three patterns, one job: choosing what the listener notices.

Pattern 1 · 2 · 3

Three ways to split

Ask what you want the listener to notice: a thing, an action, or the fact that there is nothing more.

A person or thing
It + be + … + that / who

It's a love of life that motivates them.

An action or idea
What + … + be + …

What they want is to stay safe.

The only thing
All + … + be + …

All they are thinking about is what they need to do next.

Pattern 1 in detail

It-clefts

Anything can go in the spotlight — a person, a thing, a time or a place. It is the natural way to correct someone.

It was my sister who paid for the trip, not me.
It was in 2019 that we first met. ← a time
It's the noise that I can't stand, not the work.
Patterns 2 & 3

What vs All

Both save the spotlight for the end of the sentence. All adds one extra meaning: nothing more than this.

What = the thing

What I want is a coffee.

What keeps them safe is careful planning and attention to detail.

All = the only thing

All I want is a coffee. (nothing else)

A what-clause is always singular: is, never are.

⚠ Watch out

Common mistakes

What they want to stay safe.

Correct: What they want is to stay safe.

It was my sister which paid.

Correct: it was my sister who paid.

What keeps them safe are careful planning…

Correct: what keeps them safe is careful planning…

All what I want is a coffee.

Correct: All I want is a coffee.
