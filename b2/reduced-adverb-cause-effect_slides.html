Reduced Adverb
Cause-Effect Clauses

Being metal, the lid expands more when it gets hot.

-ing · being having + past participle
The idea

Same cause, fewer words

A clause of reason can be shortened to a participle phrase. The meaning is identical — the sentence is just tighter and more formal.

Full → reduced

Because you have used her name a couple of times, you're less likely to forget it.

Having used her name a couple of times, you're less likely to forget it.

What disappears

× the conjunction

× the subject

× the tense

Which participle you use depends on the verb and the tense you started with.

Route 1 · 2 · 3

Three ways to reduce

Ask two questions: is the verb be, and did the cause happen before the result?

Most verbs
-ing form

Because I am standing in the supermarket…
→ Standing in the supermarket, I can check the photo.

The verb be
being + adjective / noun

Since it is metal…
→ Being metal, the lid expands more when it gets hot.

Perfect tenses
having + past participle

Because you have used her name…
→ Having used her name, you're less likely to forget it.

Route 3 in detail

having + past participle

Use it when the cause is in the present perfect or past perfect, or simply happened before the result.

Having lived abroad for ten years, she speaks four languages fluently.
Not having eaten since breakfast, I was starving by two o'clock.
Having been warned twice, he finally slowed down. ← passive
The golden rule

One subject only

A reduced clause has no subject of its own, so it borrows the subject of the main clause. If the two subjects differ, you cannot reduce.

Works

Since the lid is metal, it expands.

→ Being metal, the lid expands.

Doesn't work

Since the lid is metal, I waited.

→ Being metal, I waited. (I'm not metal)

⚠ Watch out

Common mistakes

Because being metal, the lid expands.

Correct: Being metal, the lid expands.

Have used her name, you're less likely to forget it.

Correct: Having used her name…

Don't having a car, we take the bus.

Correct: Not having a car…

Having already saw the film, she stayed in.

Correct: Having already seen the film…
