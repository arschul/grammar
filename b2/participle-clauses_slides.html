The idea

A clause with nothing in it

A participle clause has no subject and no tense. It borrows both from the main clause — which is why the two must share a subject.

Form 1 in detail

Four jobs for -ing

The -ing clause is always active, but it can mean four different things.

Walking in, do you pick up on the feelings there? ← at the same time
Scanning the room, she noticed who sat next to whom. ← one after the other
Not knowing much about them, I still came up with ideas. ← a reason
The room was full of negative energy, making it harder. ← a result
The golden rule

One subject only

The clause has no subject, so the reader attaches it to the subject of the main clause. Get that wrong and the sentence becomes accidentally absurd.

Works

Walking in, I felt the room was tense.

I walked in — the subjects match.

Dangles

Walking in, the room felt tense.

The room didn't walk in.

⚠ Watch out

Common mistakes

Walking in, the room felt tense.

Correct: Walking in, I felt the room was tense.

Knowing as “microexpressions,” these matter.

Correct: Known as “microexpressions”…

Having did this a few times…

Correct: Having done this a few times…

Annoyed at having called to the meeting.

Correct: at having been called to the meeting.
