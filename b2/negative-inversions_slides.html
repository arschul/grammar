The idea

Front it, then flip it

Move a negative or limiting adverbial to the front for emphasis, and the subject and auxiliary swap places — exactly as in a question.

Normal → inverted

I have never seen such chaos.

Never have I seen such chaos.

The structure

negative adverbial + auxiliary

+ subject + main verb

It is a statement, not a question. No question mark.

Rule 1

No auxiliary? Add do

If the sentence has no auxiliary, insert do / does / did in the right tense and put the main verb back into its base form.

She rarely arrives on time. → Rarely does she arrive on time.
We seldom ate out. → Seldom did we eat out.
Already have an auxiliary? Just move it: Seldom had we been so badly treated.
Rule 2

The trigger words

Never · Rarely · Seldom · Little · Only · Not only … but also · Under no circumstances · At no time

No sooner had I sat down than the phone rang.
Hardly had the concert begun when the power went out.
Under no circumstances should you give out your password.
Watch these two

Where the flip happens

Two structures don't invert where students expect them to.

Only + time / condition

The inversion is delayed to the main clause.

Only when I got home did I realise I had lost my keys.

Not only … but also

Only the first clause inverts.

Not only was the flight delayed, but we also lost our luggage.

⚠ Watch out

Common mistakes

Never I have seen such chaos.

Correct: Never have I seen such chaos.

Rarely I go to the cinema.

Correct: Rarely do I go to the cinema.

No sooner had I closed my eyes when…

Correct: …than the baby started crying.

Rarely did she arrived on time.

Correct: did she arrive on time.
