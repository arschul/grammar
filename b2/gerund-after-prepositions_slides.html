Gerunds after
Prepositions

She put on her hat and left without saying a word.

preposition + -ing never + to
The idea

A verb doing a noun's job

Prepositions are followed by nouns. A gerund is the noun form of a verb — so after any preposition, use -ing.

Examples

He managed by asking for help.

She is very good at making sculptures.

The test

good at it

→ good at making

If it fits after the word, that word is a preposition.

Pattern 1 & 2

Prepositions, one word or two

by, without, before, after, in spite of, instead of, except for, apart from

He was able to manage by asking for help.
They took a break instead of starting their design.
I like my job except for being on call at weekends.
Pattern 3 & 4

Verbs and phrasal verbs

apologise for, talk about, insist on, succeed in, give up, end up, keep on, put off

He apologised for missing the meeting.
They often talk about starting their own business.
She kept on asking, and we ended up buying it online.
The big trap

Two jobs for to

infinitive marker
to + base verb

I want to go.

want, hope, decide, need, promise

preposition
to + gerund

I look forward to going.

look forward to, be used to, object to, in addition to

He used to work nights = past habit. He is used to working nights = he's accustomed to it.

⚠ Watch out

Common mistakes

I look forward to hear from you.

Correct: I look forward to hearing from you.

She is very good at to cook.

Correct: She is very good at cooking.

He left without say goodbye.

Correct: He left without saying goodbye.

He's responsible checking the invoices.

Correct: He's responsible for checking the invoices.

Spot the mistake

I look forward to hear from you soon.

I look forward to hearing from you soon.

✓ Here to is a preposition, so it takes a gerund.

They took a break instead of start their design.

They took a break instead of starting their design.

✓ Instead of is a two-word preposition.

Thank you very much for help me with the report.

Thank you very much for helping me with the report.

✓ A verb after for must be a gerund.

He's responsible checking the invoices every month.

He's responsible for checking the invoices every month.

✓ The preposition itself is missing — responsible for.
