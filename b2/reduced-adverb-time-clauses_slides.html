Reduced Adverb
Time Clauses

After studying all day, I like to relax.

before / since / after + -ing on + -ing
The idea

Same meaning, fewer words

A time clause can be shortened to a time expression plus an -ing form. The meaning is identical — the sentence is just tighter and more formal.

Full → reduced

Before I played it, I never thought I'd enjoy it.

Before playing it, I never thought I'd enjoy it.

What disappears

× the subject

× the tense

The route depends on the time expression you start with.

Route 1

before · since · after

Keep the time expression, delete the subject, change the verb to the gerund.

After finishing her degree, she moved to Berlin.
Before signing anything, read the small print.
Since moving to the coast, I haven't had a single cold.
Route 2

as soon as → on

Delete as soon as, replace it with on, remove the subject, use the gerund.

As soon as she heard the news → On hearing the news, she phoned her mother.
As soon as we arrived → On arriving, we went straight to bed.
On + -ing means immediately after. It belongs to formal, written English.
The golden rule

One subject only

A reduced clause has no subject of its own, so it borrows the subject of the main clause. If the two subjects differ, you cannot reduce.

Works

I locked the door before I left.

→ I locked the door before leaving.

Doesn't work

Before I left, the phone rang.

→ Before leaving, the phone rang. (the phone didn't leave)

⚠ Watch out

Common mistakes

After I studying all day, I like to relax.

Correct: After studying all day…

…I never thought I'd enjoy it before to play it.

Correct: before playing it.

As soon as arriving home, we started dinner.

Correct: On arriving home…

On hear the news, he burst into tears.

Correct: On hearing the news…
