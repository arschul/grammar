Past Modals
of Deduction

The lights were off and the door was locked — they must have gone out.

modal + have + p.p. evidence → conclusion
The idea

Guessing about the past

You have evidence but not the facts. A past modal of deduction lets you state how sure you are about a finished action.

Examples

She didn't reply, so she may not have seen my message.

He was in Tokyo, so he couldn't have been at the party.

The structure

modal + have

+ past participle

have never changes for person, and never becomes had.

Certainty

must have & couldn't have

must have = it definitely happened  ·  can't / couldn't have = it definitely didn't

The road is wet — it must have rained in the night.
You can't have finished already — you only started ten minutes ago!
She was in hospital all week, so she couldn't have taken the exam.

The opposite of must have is can't have — not mustn't have.

Possibility

may · might · could have

may / might / could have = it possibly happened  ·  may not / might not have = it possibly didn't

He might have forgotten the date — he's very busy.
You could have dropped your keys in the car.
They may not have arrived yet — the traffic is terrible.

These three are interchangeable for a positive guess.

How sure are you?

The certainty scale

100% yes
must have + p.p.

She must have missed the train.

it definitely happened

maybe yes
may / might / could have + p.p.

He might have forgotten the date.

it possibly happened

maybe no
may not / might not have + p.p.

They may not have arrived yet.

it possibly did not happen

100% no
can't / couldn't have + p.p.

You can't have finished already!

it definitely did not happen

⚠ Watch out

Common mistakes

They must had left before the storm.

Correct: They must have left before the storm.

She must have went home early.

Correct: She must have gone home early.

I must of left it at home.

Correct: I must have left it at home.

He mustn't have been at the meeting.

Correct: He can't have been at the meeting.

Practice 2
tap a card to fix it

Spot the mistake

They must had left before the storm started.

They must have left before the storm started.

✓ After a modal use have, never had.

I can't find my ticket — I must of left it at home.

I must have left it at home.

✓ Of is only the sound of weak have. Write have.

He didn't recognise me, so he must forgotten my face.

He must have forgotten my face.

✓ Have is missing between the modal and the participle.

It must have rained last night — the ground is completely dry.

It can't have rained last night.

✓ Dry ground is evidence against rain, so the deduction is negative.

Recap

Remember this

The formula
modal + have + past participle
must have
it definitely happened
may / might / could have
it possibly happened
can't / couldn't have
it definitely did not happen

Never "must had", never "must of" — always "must have".
