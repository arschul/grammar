The idea

States, not actions

Some verbs describe what something is — a state. Others describe what someone does — an action. The difference changes which tense you can use.

Examples

She seems tired. → a state you can see

She is running to school. → an action she does

The rule

Stative → a state, a feeling, a thought

Dynamic → an action, a process

Stative verbs are not usually progressive.

The choice

Which tense can I use?

Stative
Use the simple form, even for right now
I don't understand this.
She wants to leave now.
Dynamic
Use simple or progressive, depending on the meaning
He runs every day.
We're playing a video game.

Two meanings

When a verb crosses over

think
state
I think she's amazing.
= this is my opinion
action
She's thinking about her work.
= it's in her head right now
have
state
They have a beach house.
= they own it
action
We're having lunch.
= we're eating it now
taste
state
This pasta tastes delicious.
= that's its flavour
action
She's tasting the soup.
= she's trying it on purpose
see
state
I see the problem.
= I understand it
action
I'm seeing the dentist at four.
= I'm meeting her
be
state
He is very polite.
= that's his personality
action
He's being polite today.
= he's behaving that way now
smell
state
The bread smells good.
= that's its smell
action
He's smelling the milk.
= he's using his nose on purpose
⚠ Watch out

Common mistakes

I am wanting a coffee.

I want a coffee.

She is knowing the answer.

She knows the answer.

This bag is belonging to Marco.

This bag belongs to Marco.

I am not agreeing with you.

I don't agree with you.
