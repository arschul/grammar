Verb + Gerund
or Infinitive

I'd like to go. I'd rather stay. I like going too.

would like/prefer + to would rather + base
Three patterns

Which form comes after?

Either (no difference)
-ing or to + verb

I hate waiting. = I hate to wait.
She likes eating out. = She likes to eat out.

Would like / prefer
always to + verb

I'd like to see it.
She'd prefer to stay.
We'd like to thank you.

Would rather
base verb (no to)

I'd rather see an action film.
They'd rather know the truth.

Like, love, hate, prefer

Both forms are fine — same meaning

I hate eating alone. = I hate to eat alone.
We prefer shopping online. = We prefer to shop online.
She likes eating out with friends. = She likes to eat out.
Would like / prefer / rather

When 'd appears — the rules change

I'd like to travel by plane. (would like + infinitive)
She'd prefer to do it herself. (would prefer + infinitive)
I'd rather see an action film. (would rather + base — NO to)
⚠ Watch out

Common mistakes

I'd like going to the cinema.

I'd like to go to the cinema.

She'd prefer staying home.

She'd prefer to stay home.

I'd rather to watch TV.

I'd rather watch TV. (no "to")

We'd like thank you all.

We'd like to thank you all.
