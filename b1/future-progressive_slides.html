Teacher cue
B1 · English Grammar

Future
Progressive

What will you be doing at this time tomorrow?

will be + -ing in progress
The idea

Already happening

The action will be in progress at a future moment — not starting, not finished, but under way.

Compare

At eight I will eat dinner. → I start at eight.

At eight I will be eating dinner. → I'm already in the middle of it.

Time markers

at this time tomorrow

this time next year

Also: in five years · at eight o'clock · when you arrive

Form

Positive, negative, question

will be + -ing  ·  won't be + -ing  ·  Will + subject + be + -ing?

People will be living in smaller homes in the future.
She will not be taking any classes next semester.
Next year at this time, he won't be living with his parents.

I will be working. They will be working. — be never moves.

In use

Three jobs it does

1 in progress:  This time tomorrow we will be flying over the Atlantic.
2 happening anyway:  I'll be seeing Tom on Friday, so I can tell him.
3 polite enquiry:  Will you be using the car tonight?

Will you use the car? sounds like a request. Will you be using it? is just a question.

The exception

Verbs that stay simple

stative verbs
know · believe · understand · want · need · like · own · be

I'll know the results by Friday.

a state, not an action

never say
will be + stative -ing

I'll be knowing the results by Friday.

She'll be owning the flat by then.

If the verb describes a state, use the simple future instead.

⚠ Watch out

Common mistakes

We will flying over the Atlantic.

Correct: We will be flying over the Atlantic.

She will be teach her evening class.

Correct: She will be teaching her evening class.

Next year he will being living abroad.

Correct: Next year he will be living abroad.

I'll be knowing the results by nine.

Correct: I'll know the results by nine.

✓ The main verb takes -ing after be.

I'll know the results by nine.
